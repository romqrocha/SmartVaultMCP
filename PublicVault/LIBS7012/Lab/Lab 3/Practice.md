# Solution
A student finishes graduate studies working on a problem that has aspects that are directly patentable and solve a major problem in the disk drive industry. 
Her new job could be with Hitachi or Seagate, or some similar firm.
She arrives at the new job and discovers that the work done as a student, which is in the patent process, will solve the problem at her new company. If she reveals what she knows to her new employer she will be an immediate hero but will compromise the patent process at her original institution. This step could have important financial implications for the original institution in the form of royalties. 
What should she do?
# Response

Conclusion: She should not reveal the details of the patent.

1. It is wrong to break promises
2. The student has promised to allow the institution to patent the new solution
3. Therefore it is wrong to share details

## Counter arguments
- Allowing the university to complete the process does not benefit the student, but sharing with the company might
	- By breaking a promise to the university, the student may damage her reputation within the company - if she's leaked secrets once, she might do it again
- By not revealing the details, the company will waste time and money on research that isn't required.
	- The company has already budgeted the research, and since sharing the details may have financial impacts for the student(in the form of a lawsuit), not sharing the details has no impact on the companies financial plans and has a greater net benefit.
