The final exam will be made up of a Learning Hub multiple choice exam and a paper exam.  Both parts of the final exam will be closed book.  The total length of the exam is 3 hours. The final exam will be on all of the material we covered in the course. Each part of the exam is worth 20% of your final course mark.

The multiple choice will be approximately 60 questions.  It will be a Learning Hub quiz. The official length of time for the multiple choice will be 1 hour, but I will allow you to use up to 90 minutes to complete it.  Realize that if you use more than 60 minutes, you will be "borrowing" time from the 2 hours allocated for the paper exam. The multiple choice exam MUST be done on the lab computer. You will need to log into the Safe Exam Browser after logging into your lab computer, and before starting the Learning Hub quiz.

The paper exam may consist of:

- questions where I provide you part of the solution to a lab or assignment that was assigned in the course and leave out a part of the solution and you must supply the missing line(s) of code.  
- true and false and/or short answer questions.  
- multiple choice questions.  
- questions where I will ask you to study some C++ code and ask you questions about the code (for example: what will the code output; identify errors in the code; make minor modifications to the code; etc.).  
- questions where I ask you to make modifications to provided code.  
- implementation of a short global functions or class member functions.  
  
If I am asking you to write or modify code, it will be code identical to, or very similar to, code that you were required to write for a lab exercise or an assignment.

On both parts of the final exam, I will be testing you on concepts that I feel you should _NOT_ have to look up in a text - in other words concepts or facts that you should have memorized or that you should be able to reason out.

The most efficient way to study for the final exam is to review/study the following (in the order specified):  
  
- Notes you took during my lectures  
- In Class Labs (and solutions I provided)  
- Assignments (and solutions I provided)  
- Example programs and the slides we went through during class time and home study materials assigned during the course.  
  
The following topics will most likely be on the paper exam where you will need to write/modify/analyze code:

dynamic memory allocation  
operator overloading  
templates  
exception handling  
move semantics  
  
There will likely be around 15 to 20 questions on the paper exam, so the above will not be the only subjects on the exam. But if you are prepared for the questions related to the topics listed above, you should do reasonably well on the paper exam.
