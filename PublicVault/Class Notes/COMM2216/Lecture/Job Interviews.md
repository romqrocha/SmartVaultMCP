- The secret is to have fun
- The interviewers are people too
	- They have a difficult decision to make
	- You can make that decision easy for them

# Understanding the interview/interviewers
- The employer needs to learn a lot about each candidate in very few minutes
- Interviews are incredibly expensive to do
- Things they're trying to check:
	1) Is your resume accurate?
	2) Do you catch on quickly and intelligently?
	3) Are you professional?
	4) Are you personable?
	5) What would you be like to work with?
- Each if different, but have most things in common
- People are going to ask stock questions
- Are looking for certain characteristics
- **Your job is to create a positive image**
- Keep in mind: You get to define who you are!
	- Communicate a deliberate image of yourself using categories and appropriate details
	- Provide a well-rounded answer to the "Tell us about yourself" using a PAWS strategy
		- P: Personal(History and friends)
		- A: Academic
		- W: Work
		- S: Social(Groups and associations)
	- Match their expectations: Research the company and think of interesting questions.
- The person who gets the job is the person who performs the best in the interview

# Preparing
- If face-to-face
	- Bring 2 copies of you resume, and your references.
	- Documents: Diplomas, degrees, certs, and above
	- Paper and pen, or pad and stylus
	- Questions for them/research
- Before you walk in:
	- You don't have to be perfect
	- They're looking for someone who can do the job
	- There will always be other jobs
	- You're checking them out as much as they're checking you out
- When you walk in:
	- Interviewers often form lasting first impressions
	- Dress for success:
		- Wear clothes that create a favorable image
		- Don't overdress!
- When you meet interviewers:
	- Smile
	- Make eye contact
	- Introduce yourself
	- Give a firm handshake
- Align with the interviewers:
	- Emphasize similarities, not differences.
	- Be positive: Never criticize or complain

- Admire current prospective employer's achievements
- Don't interrupt
- Don't fidget

# Handling Questions
- Most are entirely stock:
	- How did you perform in your last job
	- What do you like/dislike about work/school?
	- What would your friends/employers say about you?
	- What are your strengths/weaknesses?
	- What five words best describe you?
- Most start with
	- Tell us about yourself
	- Why should we hire you
	- why do you want to work for us
- These are actually the same question: They want you to talk about yourself
	- Prepare good answer beforehand; be ready to supply anecdotal proof.
	- Tell them things that will paint the best picture of you
- Be prepared to discuss your strengths and weaknesses
- Strengths:
	- Should be related to what you can do for them on the job
		- Passion for type of work
		- Awareness that product will be used by others
			- Client-focused
- Weaknesses:
	- May be unrelated to the job
		- Public speaking
		- lack of actual experience
		- Not always speaking up when you have good ideas
	- The point is not to disqualify yourself, but to see if you have an honest assessment of your abilities and see how you handle it.
