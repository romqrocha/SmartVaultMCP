- Jobs almost never go to the person who is most qualified. It goes to the person who markets and interviews best.
- The primary thing they look for is "Will you fit in"
- About half of the hiring decisions people make, they feel they decided incorrectly
# Myth of Experience Trap
- Don't worry about it.
- Its all talk.
## Plan and research your career
- Find out all you can about the industry you want to get into
	- What tech do they use
	- What tech is on the up-and-up
- Research successful companies and people
	- Use company websites, LinkedIn, etc.
- Have some idea what's happening in the industry
	- techmeme.com
	- slashdot.org
	- gamedeveloper.com
- Develop company culture
- Every company has its culture
	- Establish what the culture is:
		- Small, medium or large company?
		- What image do they project about themselves?
		- Areas of specialization
		- How would you fit in?
- Organize a contact database
	- Make a file on all contacts made, applications and companies, etc.
	- Record dates, calls made, a folder for emails, notes what you said/done
	- Record individuals/companies you meet and the projects they mention.
# Finding job openings
- Two kinds of jobs:
	1) Advertised openings
		- Most advertise on their website
		- Can also check job search sites: Monster.ca, find-a-job-canada.com, etc.
		- Subscribe to get notifications
	2) Unadvertised openings
		- Shows interest even though they're not hiring
			- Find a company you want to work for
			- Try to find who is the hiring manager is and use their name
			- "send" a resume and cover letter. This gives you an excuse to contact them again later.
			- If they mention they aren't hiring, you can follow up with
				- Do you know who is hiring
				- What can I do to make my resume better
				- What skills should I work on, etc.
		- Advantages:
			- Kept on file, so they can shortcut the hiring process when needed
			- Already have a personal connection

# Social Media Warning
- Use LinkedIn to make contacts and place queries about job openings

# Informational Interviews and Networking
- An interview between you and a more experienced person in your field
	- Mention you're graduating next year and want to do X job and ask if they'd be willing to talk to you about the job and how to get started
	- Ask when you follow up on unsolicited applications
	- Try making blind calls to companies you like
- Good way to get information, build contacts, and get leads
- Make notes about the job AND the small personal details so you can follow up with those details when you follow up with them.
- Treat it like a job interview - because it is!
- **DANGER** - when you ask for an interview, they may offer to do one immediately
	- **BE PREPARED FOR THIS**
- Don't forget you can also ask if the person you're interviewing if they know anyone else you can talk to.
- Everyone can be a contact:
	- Work contacts
	- Mentors
	- Instructors
	- Fellow students
	- Friends
- Places to go:
	- Meetups
	- Hackathons
	- Tech Industry Days
	- LinkedIn
# Market yourself consciously
- You **are** the product you are selling
- Identify your strengths and emphasize them
- Clearly emphasize how you can contribute to the company success
