# What is a proposal
- A suggestion to take an action
- A suggestion to work together
- A suggestion to commit to a plan of action, and to achieve a goal

- It is an entirely forward looking document intended to:
	- solve a problem, or
	- create an improvement
- If agreed to, it constitutes a contract

- Can be as simple as purchasing new equipment to as radical as a complete company overhaul
- Are mission critical for businesses/organizations
- Often the only way to get contracts/research funding/projects approvals

# Scales of complexity

- 2 axis of 2 each
	- who sent it
		- Internal
		- External
	- Did you know it was coming?
		- Solicited
		- Unsolicited

- Internal
	- Reader is aware of your ability/background
	- Less writing, often in memo format
- External
	- Written for an outside organization
	- Readers don't know you or your abilities
	- You have to prove you understand the problem being solved
	- Longer introduction, more thorough problem description
	- Likely a background of abilities and skills
- Solicited
	- Direct request(to spec) or RFP(request for proposal)
	- Often has strict requirements they need you to meet
- Unsolicited
	- You see an opportunity, so you make the approach
		- You're solving a problem they may not know they have
	- You have to convince them you understand their needs and your proposal is achievable
	
# Why would a someone say no?
- Change is:
	- Expensive
		- Reassure with positive ROI
	- Risky
		- Reassure with other examples
		- You can also show that the risk is **greater** if you don't update
	- Inertia
		- Change is convenient: it threatens established processes and agents
		- Reassure by explaining integration process
			- Offer training/support

# Overview
## Summary
- 1 page summary
- Why is a change necessary? What can you do? What main benefit?
## Introduction
- Offers context
- What problem or current solution is the proposal meant to solve or improve?
## Proposal

### Your solution
- What, specifically, do you propose to do and how will it work?
	- In detail
	- Readers wont be convinced by what they don't understand
- What benefits?
### Alternative solutions
- What other solutions exist?
- Why are you dismissing them?

### Schedule
- How will this be implemented?
- How will it affect the business?

## Costs
- Break it down to justify the costs.
- What's the ROI? Hold backs?

## Evaluation
- How will you measure/prove the benefits?
- How will you test/prove success?

## Qualifications
- Reassure that you are able to deliver
