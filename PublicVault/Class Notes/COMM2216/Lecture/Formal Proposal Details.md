# Summary
- Critical part
- Might be the only section read
- 1 page, covers the main elements of the entire report
- May mention qualification, if particularly impressive
- **MAY** include costs, generally does not
- Written last

# Introduction
DO NOT INCLUDE FINDINGS IN THE INTRODUCTION
- Introduces the problem/need
	- Show the reader you understand the current situation and why its a problem(cost, time, reputation)
	- Be thorough and specific so the reader *must* acknowledge that there is a problem
- Proposes a solution
- Mentions the benefits
	- How the proposal solves the problem
	- How the results will benefit reader/company
- Forecast the rest of the report
	- "The following report will detail the proposal, estimate the schedule, and abcd"
	- A "Prose outline"

# Project/Solution Details
- Technical details about solution and how it works
	- Write at a level the reader will understand
- Describe benefits
	- Match the ones in the benefits
- Length will depend on scope of proposed solution
	- Answer all the readers questions
	- Demonstrate your knowledge
	- Persuade the reader that you've thought of everything
- Specifies implementation methods and stages
	- How things will get done
	- Allows for reasonable milestones and testing
	- Discussion of complexity justifies the cost
	- Covers what needs to be implemented
		- Materials
		- Equipment
		- Labor
		- Facilities
		- Time spans
	- Often done in Conjunction with the Schedule

# Schedule
- Necessary to demonstrate how and when you'll carry out the proposal
- Make clear what the tasks are
- Do not include information in the Implementation subsection
- Shows start and end date of tasks
- show visually how tasks interact
- **Use a Gantt Chart**
# Evaluation
- For long-term projects
	- States how you'll evaluate progress and success
- For projects where success is hard to measure
	- Makes it clear how to judge completion of the proposal
- Demonstrates you are thorough and capable of making management decisions

# Costs/Budget
- Have to show the idea is economically feasible
- Good ideas are only good if affordable or provide a good ROI
- Show you can implement the proposal within budget
- As detailed as possible to show you have taken everything into account
	- Also shows you are not gouging
- Details are persuasive; more details are more persuasive
- Provide a detailed breakdown that lists everything, **including** contingency costs
	- Contingency are the unknowns, and account for non-best-case scenarios
	- The client is often the biggest cause of contingency expenses (and headaches)
	- Should also include maximum amount of changes, and clauses that allow you to start charging hourly

## Startup Costs
- What it costs to get the project running
	- Equipment,
	- Supplies
	- Materials
	- Inventory
	- Fees, 
	- Deposits,
	- Licensing,
	- etc.

## Ongoing/Maintenance fees
- Provide costs for potential future costs for the product
- Includes costs like maintenance, database fees, etc.
- Do research to provide reasonable estimates for the costs
- Provide sources for the estimates

## Income Statement
- Based on market analysis, estimate monthly sales revenues
- Revenue predictions must be reasonable
	- Provide sources
- Lists revenue and subtracting costs to determine estimated net income

# Conclusion &  Recommendations
- Brings together primary findings and recommendations from all sections of the report into a convenient location
- Ends the report positively
- Restates key benefits
- States actions

# References
- Include all legitimate sources as references
- Use APA or IEEE format
- Both citations and a reference section are required

# Appendices
- Anything that might be useful or significant, but too disruptive to put in the body - such as study findings or tables, figures, charts, and maps
