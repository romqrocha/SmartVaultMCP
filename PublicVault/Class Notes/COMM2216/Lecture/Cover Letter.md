# Overview
- Meant to put your resume over the top against limited, but close, competition
- Does not repeat the resume, but adds additional details
	- Expands on your one, two, or three best qualifications
- May introduce new material *if longer than 1 page*
- Write it as a 1 minute elevator pitch

# Format
- Like a business letter
	- 1 page
	- 1 inch margin on left and right
	- 12 pt font(serif) or 10 pt(sans-serif)
	- Use same font and letterhead from resume

## Structure
- Addressing info
	- **REALLY** important to get this correct if you can find it
		- If you use a name, include Dear and mr/ms/mrs if known, or FirstName LastName if you don't.
	- Job title will work just as well (hiring manager)
		- Dear Hiring Manager
		- Dear Recruiter
	- If you cannot find a name OR job title, just leave it out and save the space.
- Document title
	- Use the job title you're applying for
	- Centered and bolded
	- Include position number and source, if advertised
	- "Application for Web Developer Position (REF: FTR671)"
	- "Application for Junior Programmer Position (monster.com, Jan. 04)"
- Opening paragraph
- 2-3 paragraph body
- closing paragraph
- Formal close
- Signature block

# Formal Close and Signature
- Best Regards, Sincerely, Kind regards, Respectfully
- Leave space for signature above name if a paper copy
	- Don't leave a space if you aren't leaving a signature

# Content
## Opening Paragraph
- Creates a first impression - confident, competent, professional.
- Two key hooks we use:
	- Something we know about the company(What you like/know about them)
	- Summarize how you meet the most important qualifications asked for
		- Think of it like a thesis
- Should be short so you can get on to telling them more about you.
- Introduce your best features, which you will expand on *in the same order* in the body

### How to not start
1) My name is...
2) I'm writing to apply... / ...to express my interest in...
3) Saying something negative about yourself
	1) "Though I don't have the skills you need..."

# Body
- An employer is interested in what you can do for them
- Develop in detail the skills/attributes/accomplishments mentioned in the opening paragraph
- Explain how these will add value to the company
- Help them visualize you as an active, responsible, productive member of their team.

## Tips
1) Lead with your best paragraph
2) Use keywords from the job ad
3) Use concrete evidence of a skill, quantified results if possible.

# Close
- Refer them to your resume, website, Github, portfolio
- As for an interview, if an ad, or a meeting, if an unsolicited application
	- Let them know how to contact you
- Thank them for their time.

# Unsolicited Letters
- If no job is advertised, write a letter expressing your interest
- Indicate the sort of position you are interested in or think you are qualified
- Tell them what you can do
- ask for a meeting, not an interview
- Research the company more fully!

# Final guidelines
- Use their language
- Communicate something personal about yourself
- Answer the question: Why should I see you?
