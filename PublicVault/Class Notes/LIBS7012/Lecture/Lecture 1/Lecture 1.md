# Philosophy vs Science
- Science deals with empirical data; things we can observe.
- Philosophy deals with things can cannot observe or that cannot be observed with empirical research alone.
# Fields of Philosophy
- Epistemology; What is Knowledge?
- Logic; What is valid reasoning?
- Metaphysics; Nature of reality that is not physical
- Ethics; What makes an action morally right?
# Meta-Ethics
- Discussing the meanings of words and statements
# Normative Ethics
- What moral norms someone should follow
# Applied Ethics
- What someone should do in specific cases
# Writing style
> [!note] Philosophy is not giving your personal opinion
- Think about the way arguments are given in court
- The point of the argumentative essays is the persuade the reader that your statement is correct by using rational arguments
	- General advice: Give your point up-front
	- Will be evaluated on the strength of your argument and knowledge of the theories and concepts
	- The entire point is not to express your personal feelings.
	- Do not say:
		- "I believe in X" 
		- "I don't feel that X is right"
# Moral Reasoning
> [!quote] "No ought without can"
> There can be no moral requirement to do something unless it is possible to do

>[!quote] No ought from is
>Moral values cannot be established from empirical facts.
>This means every moral argument must build on a mutual understanding of some moral value.

> [!quote] Explanation is not justification
> Simply explaining why something happened does not justify the actons

# Arguments
- Persuasive structures containing statements with truth values: One or more premises and a conclusion
## Premise
- Each premise is a **claim**
- Each claim can be either valid, in structure, or true
## Validity
> [!note] Validity is about the **structure** of an argument.
> It has nothing to do with the accuracy or truthiness of the argument or conclusion
- Three ways to define:
	1. If the conclusion follows from the premises
## Soundness
- Sound IAOI 
	1. The argument is valid
	2. All premises are true
> [!question] Why do we say "If and only if"?
> Because they are not just necessary *but also* sufficient for an argument to be sound.
# Types of claims
## Value Claim
> [!note] Is there a claim of approval/approval?
- When someone can disagree with the statement
	- "x is *exciting"
	- "y is *wrong*"
## Empirical Claims
> [!note] Can we use sensory tests alone to determine the truth of the claim?
