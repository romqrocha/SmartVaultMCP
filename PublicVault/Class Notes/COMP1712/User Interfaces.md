UI is all the hardware, software, menus, function, etc., that enable two-way communication between the user and the computer.

  

Usability is a blanket term that contains several sub-topics:

- Effectiveness
    - Does it to the right thing? Does it get it done?
- Efficiency
    - Does it do it with minimum mental and physical effort
- Satisfaction
    - Does it feel good to use?

  

A _usable_ interface should be

- easy to become familiar and competent with during the **first use**
- Easy to achieve objectives in
- Easy to recall for subsequent visits

  

The best interfaces are the ones the users don’t notice

  

## The Process of Designing User Interfaces

- Understand the User
- Organize the Interface
- Define Standards
- Develop Prototypes

  

**Human Centered Design** argues that when we use words that make a person sound like a component, such as ‘user’, we are dehumanizing them.

In order to maintain the humanity of a user, and organize their goals and requirements, we use **Personas**

The key feature of User Centered Design is **direct engagement with real users** to ensure the solution words in the real context of use, and not just the one we were imagining.

  

  

## Evaluating

Heuristic Evaluation:

- Compare to a set of predefined heuristics/principles

Walk-through Evaluation:

- The project team presents the prototype

Interactive Evaluation:

- The users themselves work with HTML or language prototypes

Formal usability testing

- Only used with language prototype. In a special lab equipped with cameras and special software to record keystrokes and mouse operations.

  

# 8 Golden Rules of Interface Design

1. Strive for ==**Consistency**==
    1. The sequence of actions in similar circumstances should be similar to other apps and other places in your system
    2. Identical terminology in prompts/menus/etc.
    3. Consistent color, layout, capitalization, fonts
    4. Exceptions should be understandable and limited in number
2. Seek ==**Universal Usability**==
    1. Recognize the needs of diverse users, such as users with disabilities, age differences, technological diversity, etc.
3. Offer informal ==**Feedback**==
4. Design dialogs to yield ==**Closure**==
    1. Sequential actions should be organized into groups with a beginning, middle, and end.
    2. Informative feedback during the completion of a process
    3. Loading bars, spinners, etc.
5. Prevent ==**Errors**==
6. Permit easy ==**Reversal**== of actions
7. Keep users in ==**Control**==
    1. Users want to feel in control or in charge of the interface, and that the interface responds to **them**.
    2. They don’t want surprises or changes in familiar UI.
    3. They are annoyed by tedious data entry, difficulty getting the information they want, or if they are unable to produce the desired result.
8. Reduce short-term ==**Memory Load**==
