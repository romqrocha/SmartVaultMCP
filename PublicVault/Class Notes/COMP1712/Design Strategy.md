# The Design Phase

- Decide if you want to **buy** or **Build** the system
    - Show the comparison of possible options
    - show how you came to the decision
- Arrange the components to develop an **architectures**
    - What hardware/software?
    - What will the interface look like?
    - Do you want a physical process model?
    - Code structure?
    - Database Structure
    - Architecture and performance requirements.

## System Acquisition Phase

- Once you enter the Design Phase, you don’t **need** to be the one to build it.
    - You could buy it
    - You could hire someone to build it
    - Regardless, you must know what you want so you can find the best option.
- Primary approaches:
    
    ### Custom Development
    
    - You get exactly what you want
    - It will be consistent with the tech and standards you already have.
    - Allows you to build, train, and retain technical skills and knowledge in-house.
    - Gives the team flexibility and autonomy over how the system works.
    - Your unique solution might provide a unique advantage over other solutions.
    
    ### Packaged System
    
    - If your processes are standardized, there is likely pre-existing software you can use
        - e.g. Payroll
    - This is rarely a perfect fit, and you must fit your business to the software
        - This can mean that you change something small, or something **substantial**
    - This also means that you are tying a process in your business to an external company existing
    - The search for a software package should be based on the requirements defined during analysis. The analysis is still essential. **The more detailed your analysis is, the better your odds are at finding the best software package**.
    - Its not uncommon for companies to hire someone to handle the **integration** of data from different software packages.
    
    ### Outsourcing
    
    - Three types:
        
        1. Application Service Provider (ASP)
            - Experts in hosting and managing other software packages, like a company who handles the setup and installation of Adobe Suite for a company
            - Bills per-use, monthly, or annually
        2. Software as a Service (SaaS)
            - The companies product is software, and they provide the software to you.
        3. Developing a custom system on behalf on a customer
            - Not prepackaged, but they will develop a custom piece of software based on your requirements.
            - Risks:
                - You might need to disclose private/confidential information.
                - You may lose control over future development.
                - Expertise is being created outside your organization.
            - **MOST IMPORTANT RISKS**
                1. Assess the requirements thoroughly - **you should not outsource what you do not understand**.
                2. **Choose the provider carefully** based on a proven track record with the type of system and tech your system needs.
        
        ### Types of contracts
        
        - Time and arrangement
            - You agree to pay for whatever time and expenses are needed
            - Flexible, but can result in a large bill
            - **best when you and the provider are both unclear about what it will take to complete the job**
        - Fixed-Price
            - If provider spends more than the agreed amount, the provider absorbs the cost
        - Value-added
            - Some percent of the completed system benefits are paid to the provider
            - Growing in popularity, quite low-risk for the client.
        
        ### The art of fair contracts
        
        - Need to balance **flexibility** with **clearly defined terms**.
        - Needs change over time, so **short-term contracts** enable both parties to reassess frequently, or break off the relationship if its not working out.

### **Criteria**

- Business need
    - If the need is central to the company strategy - it is usually better to not outsource so you can retain exclusive control over the project.
- In-house experience
- Project skills
    - If the skills required to make the project are not core to your business, outsourcing or purchasing allows your employees to focus on developing other business-critical skills.
- Project management
- Time frame

### Seeking additional information

- **RFP - Request For Proposal**
    - You can ask a vendor, developer, or service provider to submit a proposal
    - Describes in detail:
        - The system or services that are needed
        - Special technical needs/circumstances
        - Evaluation criteria
        - Procedures to follow
        - Timetable
    - Vendors respond by describing in detail how they:
        - supply those needs
        - The schedule
        - The timetable
    - The Vendor proposal is a B**inding Offer**
- **RFI - Request for Information**
    - Less detailed than a RFP
    - Generally asks about a vendors products and services
- **RFQ - Request for Quote**
    - When you want a vendor to simply give you a price for a given project
    - Usually used for hardware rather than software, since its easy to have a finite number and scope.
- Once you collect all the information, you make an **Alternative Matrix**
    - You need to compare the **same kind** of acquisition
        - e.g. if purchasing a package, you’d want to compare Amazon, Shopify, Square, etc.
    - Usually a **weighted** matrix
        - Weights should equal 100
