Why write at all?

- To inform
- To remind
- To record
    - To prove you communicated
- To request

Broadly, To Inform (inform, remind, record) or To Request

We want to let the reader know something they didn’t know before, or do something they didn’t know they had to do

## Letters

- Return address on top, so they don’t lose it
- UNAMBIGUOUS DATE - Spell the month and year out
- Full mailing address
- Former greeting
    - CLEAR DESCRIPTIVE BOLD TITLE
- No first line indent
- Left-justified, right ragged
- Double space between paragraphs
- IF
    - multiple pages, page count
    - Header after the title page
        - Lists name of report/addressee, date
- Ends with formal closure
    - Sincerely
    - Regards
    - Cheers and Thanks
        - Less formal, good for emails
- Signature block
    - Name
    - title
    - contact info
- Copy line - so reader can see who else is reading it
- Enclosure line - lists items you’ve enclosed

## Memos

- Usually addressed to a more general audience
- Placed with other documents for information
- Printed hard copy
    - Generally signed, so people know its legitimate
    - Don’t have salutations or signatures, since they’re not address to a single person
    - Traditionally used a subject line, this is hard to read
        - Use a header instead

## Emails

- Will be most of the writing you will do
- Letters are still used formally, such as
    - Resignations
    - requests for promotions
    - raises
    - complaints
- Salutations and close tend to be less formal
- You don’t need Enclosure/Copy

### Writing Style

- Biggest issues
    1. Doesn’t get to the point → Start with the main idea every time
    2. Unclear/Wastes your time → Be clear and concise
    3. It isn’t friendly or personable
        1. Emails are written FROM you TO another person. Its considered a conversation.
- Always
    1. start with a SUMMARY of the main idea
        1. “What do you want?”
        2. “How can I help you?”
    2. Give Context
        1. “Why are you telling me this?”
    3. Details
        1. “Okay, so tell me exactly what happened/what you want/need” → Provide details on main idea
    4. Next Step
        1. “What have you done?”
        2. “What happens now?”
- [I want you to know\I want to tell you that…] Your bid came in considerably higher than the competitions, so we won’t be able to….
- After that rework to make it kinder/more personal
- Always consider who you’re writing to/their perspective
- Avoid sounding condescending/negative/angry
- Don’t use archaic language
- If you wouldn’t say it in conversation, don’t say it in an email.

### Levels of formality

- Letters
- Memos
- Emails
    - Client
    - Manager
    - Colleague

Ultimately, just imagine you’re speaking TO the person, and write as you would speak to them.

# More Grammar

- Every verb requires an actor
    - A “dangling verb” is a verb that does not have an agent
    - Adverb needs to be directly before or after the verb it means to modify!
    - English is _positional_ - the nearest agent acts on the verb
