- English has a bad knack of over-qualifying
    - By being excessively polite, we often change the meaning or sound unsure.
- Don’t use pat/pad phrases
    - e.g.
    - “At this juncture in time”
    - “It would behoove us”
    - “To respond in kind”
    - “To the email of”
    - NOTHING HAS BEEN SAID
- Do in this order:
    1. Find the real subject
    2. find the real verb
    3. edit for conciseness
    4. edit for clarity

## Find the real subject

- the topic of the sentence  
    or  
- The main agent(The thing that is doing the verb)
- Work best when the topic and agent are the same
- The agent should come before the action in a sentence

## Find the real verb

- Ask yourself what the subject is doing
- Sometimes the agent just is - the car is blue
- Weak expletive
    - “it is”, “there are”
    - Weak subjects and verbs when used to start a sentence in **most** cases
- Active Voice is strong, direct, and professional. It takes responsibility.
    - “Three bugs were found during the review” vs “We/I found three bugs during the review”
- Passive voice is not more technical or objective.
- We use passive when we **don’t** know or care who the agent is, or are trying to **hide who the agent is**.
    - This means using the passive means you don’t know, don’t care, or are trying to hide something.

## Edit for conciseness

- Don’t use redundant phrases
    - “We can’t proceed at the present time”
        - The present is the time
        - “We cant proceed at present”

## Edit for clarity

- Always simplify, if possible
- Technical jargon should be used, in context.

  

- On average, your sentences will be no longer than 20 words
- Paragraphs should be no more than 7 lines
