6. Describe and compare the design methodologies used for software design: waterfall, iterative, prototype, spiral, agile and scrum.
	1. waterfall: starts with detailed requirements gathering then goes through design and implementation phases before maintenance phase.
		1. needs very clear requirements
		2. takes a long time to produce deliverable
	2. iterative
		1. make an initial plan
		2. design and throwaway prototypes, learning from each
		3. deploy the winner
	3. spiral
		1. like iterative with more focus on deliberate planning instead of reactionary adjustments
	4. agile
		1. produce deployable components quickly
	5. scrum
		1. divide work into sprints
7. What are the key differences between plan-driven and agile methodologies?
	1. plan-driven is about spending more time on planning to thoughtfully consider different use cases whereas agile is more about making an MVP and deciding how to proceed based on what works well and what doesn't. 
8. What is software engineering?
	1. discipline of computer science concerned with design, development and maintenance of software applications
9. What are the principles of software engineering? Describe.
	1. must be viewed with stronger ties to computer science than other engineering fields
	2. must share common characteristic with other engineering disciplines
	3. must apply engineering methods and practices to the development of extensible software
	4. must integrate with principles of math and CS and engineering to create extensible software
	5. must include secure code standards and quality control
	6. must emphasize communication, teamwork and professional best practices
